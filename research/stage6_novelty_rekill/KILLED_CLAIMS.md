# Killed Claims

Stage: 6R Proposition-Level Novelty Re-Kill
Date: 2026-09-03

The following claims are dead as independent theory contributions under the current frozen model. They may appear as background/model implications only if clearly not presented as novelty.

## K1 — Hybrid human/AI organization is a novel result

**Killed.** Partial outsourcing/make-and-buy is established in the sourcing literature, and partial agentic-AI delegation/human-in-the-loop governance is directly modeled in current AI-governance theory.

## K2 — Full automation can be suboptimal despite current AI dominance because human capability must be preserved

**Killed as a main contribution.** Gray, Tomlin & Roth (2009) directly show internal production can be retained despite a current cost disadvantage because outsourcing affects learning and future costs. Anderson & Parker (2002) establish the dynamic learning loss from outsourcing and partial-outsourcing dominance. Current AI-learning papers establish analogous current-automation versus future-human-capability trade-offs.

## K3 — External platform unavailability by itself rescues P2 novelty

**Killed.** Concurrent-sourcing theory already makes in-house capability valuable under all-or-nothing outsourced-supply disruption. In the present model `lambda*nu` simply scales the future value of retained capability; there is no new outage-capability strategic interaction.

## K4 — Platform pricing creates a new organizational distortion theorem

**Killed under the current exogenous-price formulation.** The model defines a resource-cost benchmark by replacing `p` with `r`, so `DeltaT-DeltaP=n(p-r)` and `PhiT-PhiP=sn(p-r)` are identities. Make-and-buy theory already treats supplier markups as a reason to preserve internal sourcing options.

## K5 — Longer agentic execution necessarily increases the cutoff distortion

**Killed / unsupported.** The proof establishes that the direct price/objective wedge increases with `n`; it does not establish that `s_T^*-s_P^*` is globally increasing in `n`. Numerical counterexamples exist within an admissible concave recovery specification.

## K6 — Multi-step execution `n` is a core new mechanism

**Killed.** Setting `n=1` leaves the core P1–P4 structure intact. `n` changes success compounding and scales payments but does not alter the strategic-feedback network.

## K7 — Uniform positive human-authority persistence is independently novel because it is written as an epsilon bound

**Killed as a main contribution.** The exact epsilon statement is a valid mathematical strengthening inside the current model, but it follows from monotonicity and a sufficient condition at maximal capability. Its economic mechanism is the same current-automation/future-capability trade-off already present in close AI-learning and dynamic-outsourcing theories.

## K8 — “Nobody combined AI + recovery capability + platform pricing” is a novelty defense

**Killed by workflow rule.** Combination novelty is insufficient unless the full architecture creates a new strategic interaction or theorem unavailable in the nested benchmarks.

## K9 — The current paper can proceed to journal positioning because no exact prior game was found

**Killed.** Stage 6 v1.1 explicitly requires at least one independent theorem/mechanism/welfare result to survive. Exact whole-game nonidentity alone does not satisfy the gate.

## Claims that remain permissible as non-novel model statements

- The current model has a unique cutoff under its assumptions.
- Retained human authority can preserve a recovery-relevant experience stock.
- Under stated conditions, the firm can rationally retain humans even when AI has a current task-level advantage.
- A private access price above resource cost reduces delegation relative to the defined technological benchmark.
- The direct per-step pricing wedge scales with execution length.
- A sufficient maximal-capability condition can bound the optimal AI share away from one.

These statements may be used only as model results unless a future earlier-stage pivot creates new theory.
