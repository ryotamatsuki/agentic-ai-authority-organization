## A. Dynamic outsourcing / make-or-buy / learning

### Anderson, Edward G. Jr. and Geoffrey G. Parker (2002)

- Title: **The Effect of Learning on the Make/Buy Decision**
- Outlet: *Production and Operations Management*, 11(3), 313–339
- DOI: `10.1111/j.1937-5956.2002.tb00189.x`
- Status: published article
- Verified source: Sage/Wiley publisher record
- Relevant result: outsourcing decisions create path dependence; immediate outsourcing benefit can produce higher long-run costs; a small outsourced fraction can dominate complete insourcing and complete outsourcing.
- Overlap: dynamic internal learning and partial outsourcing.
- Classification: `STRUCTURALLY VERY CLOSE`.
- Access depth: publisher abstract / bibliographic record and indexed full-text metadata; not all equations reconstructed during this gate.

### Gray, John V.; Brian Tomlin; Aleda V. Roth (2009)

- Title: **Outsourcing to a Powerful Contract Manufacturer: The Effect of Learning-by-Doing**
- Outlet: *Production and Operations Management*, 18(5), 487–505
- DOI: `10.1111/j.1937-5956.2009.01024.x`
- Status: published article
- Relevant structure: two-period game between OEM and powerful contract manufacturer; both learn by doing.
- Relevant results: partial outsourcing can be optimal; outsourcing can vary over time; OEM may produce internally when at a current cost disadvantage (“production for leverage”).
- Overlap: strongest direct threat to current P2.
- Difference: strategic supplier learning/pricing is richer than current passive platform; no explicit external-supply outage/recovery state.
- Classification: `STRUCTURALLY VERY CLOSE`.
- Access depth: publisher full-text landing page/abstract plus bibliographic details; abstract directly states the key relevant results.

### Loertscher, Simon and Michael H. Riordan (2019)

- Title: **Make and Buy: Outsourcing, Vertical Integration, and Cost Reduction**
- Outlet: *American Economic Journal: Microeconomics*, 11(1), 105–123
- DOI: `10.1257/mic.20160347`
- Status: published article
- Relevant structure: downstream customer can source internally; integration can avoid supplier markups but discourages independent supplier cost-reduction investment.
- Overlap: internal sourcing option, supplier markup, make-and-buy organization.
- Difference: strategic supplier investment/market structure; no current recovery stock or outage state.
- Classification: `COMPONENT OVERLAP`, with strong relevance to P3 framing.
- Access depth: AEA official abstract/metadata.

### Make-or-buy strategies in a multi-stage manufacturing process and the role of learning effect in relocation decisions (2023/24 publication record)

- Verified source: *Computers & Industrial Engineering* / ScienceDirect record, article identifier associated with DOI `10.1016/j.cie.2023.109259`.
- Relevant result from indexed full text: learning can make in-house production rational even when outsourcing is more cost-efficient in the short run; full outsourcing can prevent productivity knowledge from accumulating.
- Overlap: further evidence that “retain internal activity despite current outsourcing advantage because internal activity builds future knowledge” is not AI-specific.
- Classification: `STRUCTURALLY VERY CLOSE` at mechanism level; not used as the primary predecessor because the older Anderson/Gray papers already establish the core point.

## B. Concurrent sourcing / disruption / backup capacity

### Cassidey, Thomas B.; Nickolas Freeman; Sharif Melouk (2022)

- Title: **Leveraging concurrent sourcing for risk mitigation and pricing**
- Outlet: *Omega*, 113, 102723
- DOI: `10.1016/j.omega.2022.102723`
- Status: published article
- Relevant structure: manufacturer may make and buy; outsourced supply has all-or-nothing disruption risk; manufacturer has in-house production capability; supplier pricing is analyzed.
- Relevant results: concurrent sourcing provides risk mitigation; in-house capability can induce lower supplier prices; increasing internal capacity can affect outsourcing.
- Overlap: external supplier unavailability + internal backup capability + pricing.
- Difference: internal capacity is not generated by a prior retained-authority experience stock in the same way as current `H(s)`.
- Classification: `STRUCTURALLY VERY CLOSE`.
- Access depth: ScienceDirect abstract/highlights and RePEc bibliographic record.

### Freeman, Nickolas; John Mittenthal; Burcu Keskin; Sharif Melouk (2018)

- Title: **Sourcing strategies for a capacitated firm subject to supply and demand uncertainty**
- Outlet: *Omega*, 77, 127–142
- DOI: `10.1016/j.omega.2017.06.004`
- Status: published article
- Relevant structure: unreliable supplier, in-house production, dual sourcing, capacity constraints.
- Relevant result: in-house component production is an effective way to mitigate supply/demand uncertainty.
- Classification: `COMPONENT OVERLAP` / predecessor to Cassidey et al.
- Access depth: ScienceDirect/IDEAS abstract and section snippets.

### Cassidey, Thomas B.; Nickolas K. Freeman; Sharif H. Melouk; Arunachalam Narayanan (2026)

- Title: **Concurrent sourcing behavior and bounded rationality under capacity constraints**
- Outlet: *European Journal of Operational Research*, 331(1), 229–241
- DOI: `10.1016/j.ejor.2025.09.037`
- Publication date in ScienceDirect record: 16 May 2026
- Status: published article
- Relevance: confirms current frontier continues to treat all-or-nothing outsourced-supplier disruption and contingent in-house plans as a standard concurrent-sourcing architecture.
- Classification: `MERELY RELATED` to the theory novelty question because its main contribution is behavioral/experimental rather than the current dynamic capability mechanism.

## C. AI automation / learning / deskilling / human capital

### Garicano, Luis and Luis Rayo (2025; revised 2026)

- Current CEPR title: **Training in the Age of AI: A Theory of Career Viability**
- CEPR Discussion Paper: DP20634
- Initial publication: 10 September 2025
- Current CEPR revision shown: 2 March 2026
- Kellogg page also displays an earlier/alternate title ending **A Theory of Apprenticeship Viability**.
- Status: working paper / CEPR discussion paper
- Relevant mechanism: AI automates junior tasks that finance and generate training; AI capability threatens the apprenticeship/career pipeline.
- Relevant result: viability depends on a threshold statistic; below the threshold, AI advances can threaten career/training collapse.
- Classification: `COMPONENT OVERLAP / STRUCTURALLY CLOSE AI-learning threat`.
- Deduplication note: CEPR and Kellogg records refer to the same research project; use current CEPR record for title/status.

### Afrouzi, Hassan; Andres/Andrés Blanco; Andres Drenik; Erik Hurst (2026)

- Title: **Automation, Learning, and Career Dynamics**
- NBER Working Paper: 35157, April 2026, DOI `10.3386/w35157`
- FRB Atlanta Working Paper: 2026-6, May 2026, DOI `10.29338/wp2026-06`
- Status: working paper; NBER and FRB Atlanta versions are duplicate/circulating versions of the same paper.
- Relevant structure: continuous-time GE; learning-by-doing jointly determined with automated task share and career transition.
- Relevant result: cheaper automation can push a low-learning equilibrium into a human-capital trap; planner problem explicitly accounts for automation and frontier maintenance.
- Overlap: current AI performance versus future human capability trade-off.
- Difference: macro/general-equilibrium career model, no external platform outage/price.
- Classification: `STRUCTURALLY VERY CLOSE` at mechanism level.

### Ide, Enrique (2025; revised June 2026)

- Title: **Automation, AI, and the Intergenerational Transmission of Knowledge**
- CEPR Discussion Paper: DP20940, published 16 December 2025, revised 8 June 2026
- SSRN/IESE record: posted July 2025, last revised 7 June 2026
- Status: working paper; versions deduplicated.
- Relevant structure: task-based overlapping-generations model; novices acquire tacit knowledge by working with experts.
- Relevant result: better entry-level automation can increase current output while reducing long-run growth/welfare by slowing knowledge transmission.
- Classification: `STRUCTURALLY VERY CLOSE` to the “automation erodes experience stock” channel, although not to external platform dependence.

### Acemoglu, Daron; Dingwen Kong; Asuman Ozdaglar (2026)

- Title: **AI, Human Cognition and Knowledge Collapse**
- NBER Working Paper: 34910
- Issue date: February 2026
- DOI: `10.3386/w34910`
- Status: working paper
- Relevant structure: dynamic learning/decision model; agentic AI substitutes for costly human context-specific learning; human learning also contributes to a shared knowledge stock.
- Relevant result: agentic AI can improve contemporaneous decisions while eroding learning incentives sustaining long-run knowledge.
- Classification: `STRUCTURALLY VERY CLOSE` to current contemporaneous-AI-gain / future-capability-loss mechanism; distinct collective-knowledge externality.

### Ide, Enrique and Eduard Talamàs (2025)

- Title: **Artificial Intelligence in the Knowledge Economy**

- Relevance: close to current routine/exception AI task architecture but does not contain the same dynamic recovery-capability result.

### Srivastava, Abhishek (2026)

- Title: **Governing AI-enabled decision making: Delegation, autonomy, and control**
- Outlet: *Production and Operations Management*, OnlineFirst
- First published online: 17 July 2026
- DOI: `10.1177/10591478261473004`
- Status: published OnlineFirst article
- Relevant structure: firm chooses among human control, full AI autonomy, and human-in-the-loop governance under operational uncertainty; AI investment can be endogenous.

- Relevance: direct threat to novelty of “hybrid AI authority” as a result.
- Difference: no current external-platform experience-stock mechanism.
- Classification: `STRUCTURALLY VERY CLOSE` to P1/hybrid-governance claim.

## E. Evidence limitations

1. The Stage 6 gate relied primarily on official publisher, NBER, FRB, CEPR, SSRN, AEA, and author/institutional records available on 2026-09-03.
2. Full model equations and appendices were not accessible for every paywalled paper. Where theorem-level detail was unavailable, the ledger does not claim exact theorem identity; it classifies overlap based on publisher-stated model structure and results.
3. This is why no paper is classified as `EXACT PRIOR ART` for the whole current game.
4. The `NO-GO` verdict instead rests on the stronger workflow point that the current full architecture produces no identified full-model-only interaction theorem beyond results already established in the close benchmark literatures.
